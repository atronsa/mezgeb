Also: a reversal or adjustment with a reason of the wrong kind is refused
(engine, and `23503` from the database); an adjustment without an approver

back exactly; INV-25 corruption is detected by `check_invariants`. All 13
schema tests, 8 idempotency tests, the concurrency tests and the
differential test still pass, the last now with random adjustments
(including self-approved ones and wrong reasons) compared between the
reference ledger and PostgreSQL.

## Acceptance criteria

**The original rows' bytes are unchanged after reversal**, verified by a
hash of each row (`md5(row_to_json(...))`) of the original entry and its
two lines, before and after the reversal:

```
original rows before and after reversal:
["5fdd93488a5c83d1bc1b85256d078597", "d119779790dd4d574c7ba5ba3e04b3e3", "85c1f0730e033f75f7a80ab25a62b53a"]
```

Identical before and after. **Pass.**

## Exit gate

**Gate:** the adjustment control is reviewed against the threat model's
insider-admin entries.

| Threat | What the adjustment controls now do | What is still open | Step |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TM-12** An insider posts an adjustment crediting an account they control | An adjustment cannot post without a reason from the adjustment list and two **different** actors. Enforced by the engine and, independently, by a database CHECK and foreign key, so bypassing the engine does not help. The adjustment, its reason and both actors are recorded in the journal, which cannot be edited (INV-9). | **Actor IDs are asserted by the client, not proven.** A compromised client, or one person with access to it, can name two different actors. Proving identity, and checking that the approver is allowed to approve, is authentication and authorisation. **No limits per person** on how much one actor can propose or approve. **No separate audit events** beyond the journal row. | 2.08 (identity, roles, limits), 2.09 (audit events) |
| **TM-12** Two insiders collude, one as maker and one as checker | Both names are recorded permanently with the entry, so collusion leaves evidence. | Two-person control cannot stop two people who agree to steal. Accepted risk, reduced by limits, alerts on adjustment volume and reconciliation. | 2.08, 2.15, 3.09 |
| **TM-11** An insider edits or deletes the journal to hide an adjustment | Unchanged and still holding: journal rows cannot be updated or deleted (trigger and privileges). Verified again: a reversal leaves the original rows byte for byte unchanged. | The hash chain, so that even a superuser's edit is detectable. | 2.09 |
| **TM-13** An insider reopens a closed period to change an adjustment after the fact | Not affected yet: periods do not exist. | Closed periods and their maker-checker reopening. | 2.05, 2.08 |
| **TM-15** An insider disables verification so a bad adjustment goes unnoticed | `check_invariants` (reference ledger) and the integrity report (database) already check INV-25 and the books; nothing runs them on a schedule yet. | The verification job and its alert. | 1.13, 3.09 |

**Conclusion.** The ledger now refuses any adjustment that lacks a reason or
a second, different approver, at two independent layers, and records who
did what in an append-only journal. Within the ledger, a single actor cannot
create money alone. What the ledger cannot yet guarantee is that the two
names belong to two real, authorised people: that is the main remaining gap
for TM-12, and step 2.08 closes it. The threat model's TM-12 row now points
to this review.

**Result: passed.**
