## Exit gate

**Question:** without notes, why does a customer deposit *credit* a liability
and *debit* an asset?

**First attempt:**

> When Abebe is putting 1,000 into the bank, the bank promises that Abebe can
> use that 1,000 from this wallet later. So two things happen simultaneously.
> First, the bank account increases by 1,000: for example, before Abebe's
> deposit they had 100,000, now they have 101,000. The bank account is an
> asset, and assets increase through debit. Second, the bank now owes Abebe
> 1,000. The wallet is a liability because the bank owes Abebe that value,
> and liabilities increase through credit.

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bank (asset) | 1,000 | 0 |
| Abebe's wallet (liability) | 0 | 1,000 |
| **Total** | **1,000** | **1,000** |

**Feedback:** the journal entry and both reasons were correct. One error:
the answer said the *bank* owes Abebe. The books are the **platform's**. The
bank only holds the platform's account, whose balance is the platform's
asset, and it is the **platform** that owes Abebe the money.

**Second attempt:**

> When Abebe is putting 1,000 into the platform, the platform promises that
> Abebe can use that 1,000 from this wallet later. So two things happen
> simultaneously. First, the platform's account increases by 1,000: for
> example, before Abebe's deposit they had 100,000, now they have 101,000.
> The platform's account is an asset, and assets increase through debit.
> Second, the platform now owes Abebe 1,000. The wallet is a liability
> because the platform owes Abebe that value, and liabilities increase
> through credit.

**Result: passed.** The second answer names the right owner of each side:
the platform owns the cash (asset, debit) and owes Abebe (liability,
credit). Both answers are lightly edited for punctuation only.

**Point to keep in mind:** a deposit is never revenue. The platform earns
nothing, so equity does not change; the 1,000 still belongs to Abebe.
