## Acceptance criteria

**After 10k random deposit/withdrawal lifecycles, clearing =
SUM(in-flight withdrawals).**
`clearing_equals_the_withdrawals_in_flight_after_10k_lifecycles` runs
10,000 complete withdrawal lifecycles (about a quarter rejected), mixed
with matched and unmatched deposits and suspense resolutions, across 8
customers and one merchant. After **every** step:

- the sum of the open `9030` items equals the sum of the withdrawals in
  flight, and the sum of the open `2020` items equals the withdrawals
  requested and not yet sent.

At the end, across every account:

- 9030 = withdrawals in flight, 2020 = withdrawals pending, 9010 =
  unresolved suspense;
- every finished withdrawal's two sub-accounts are at zero;
- 1010 = deposits − confirmed payouts, and 1010 = 2010 + 2020 + 2030 + 9010
  + 9030 exactly (no fees yet).

Along the way, more than 1,000 steps that break a lifecycle are tried: a
second confirmation, a rejection after an outcome, a partial send, and a
new request into a finished withdrawal's sub-account. Every one is refused
and posts nothing. The run takes about 4 seconds in a debug build.

## Exit gate

**The suspense and clearing account policies are documented in the
accounting model (`docs/accounting/`).** They are in
[clearing-and-suspense.md](../accounting/clearing-and-suspense.md): the
accounts, six rules, each lifecycle with its templates and worked entries,
and the ageing buckets. The worked entries use them:

- E01's open questions about clearing and unmatched deposits, and E05's
  about unmatched returns, are decided and linked to the policy.
- E03 is now two entries (sent, then confirmed) and E04 three (requested,
  sent, rejected by the bank), through `9030:wdt-…`.
- E19 and E20 book an unidentified deposit and its resolution.
- The trial balance is recomputed after E20 (28 tables, 10,655.60 posted on
  each side), and `worked_entries.rs` and the engine's `posting.rs` replay
  the new story to it.

## Why a withdrawal is not a ledger hold

The step lists `withdrawal_request.v1` as a hold. Step 1.14's holds were
considered and not used, because a hold **expires by itself** and returns
the money. Once a payout may already be with the bank, an automatic return
would pay the customer twice. Instead:

- the request is an ordinary posting into the withdrawal's own
  `2020:wd-…` sub-account, which stops the money being spent, exactly as a
  hold would (E02);
- each later step moves the whole item, and none of these accounts may go
  below zero, so the ledger itself enforces one outcome per withdrawal;
- a withdrawal stuck too long appears in the ageing report, and a person
  decides.

## What a template check does not guarantee

A template reads balances when the draft is made, outside the posting
transaction. Two consequences, both documented on `Template::book`:

- **Whole-balance checks (2014) are backed by the posting rules.** A second
  outcome drafted before the first posted passes 2014 but is refused by
  1012 when it posts, because the item is already at zero. The test above
  shows this.
- **The new-account check (2015) is not.** Two different requests drafted
  at the same moment into the same new sub-account would both pass it, and
  both post. The client must give each withdrawal and suspense item its own
  sub-account ID (for example, the withdrawal's own UUID), and the
  idempotency key stops a retried request posting twice. Reconciliation
  (step 2.15) would show such a double request.
