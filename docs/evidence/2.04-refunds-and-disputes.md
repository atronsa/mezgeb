## Implementation tasks

| Task | Done |
|---|---|
| The refund template references `original_entry_id`; the engine locks the original's refund tally and enforces `sum(refunds) ≤ original` | Yes, and per account rather than one total. A refund names its original in `EntryDraft::refunds`. The engine locks the original's tally rows, one per account, checks the refund, posts it and updates the tallies in one transaction. The limits come from the original's own lines, so the client cannot state them. The cap covers the payer, the merchant and the fee: money goes back only to whoever paid, and never more than the original moved on any account |
| `refund_tallies` table, updated in the same transaction | Yes: migration 0006 |
| `dispute_open.v1` (a hold on the merchant), `dispute_resolve_payer.v1`, `dispute_resolve_merchant.v1` | Yes. The "hold" is a per-dispute `2060:dsp-…` sub-account, not a ledger hold, because a hold expires by itself (ADR-0015). A decision for the payer is a refund of the disputed payment |
| Refund vs reversal (ADR) | Yes: [ADR-0015](../adr/0015-refunds-and-disputes.md). A reversed entry cannot be refunded, a refunded entry cannot be reversed, and a refund is never reversed |
| Fee policy on refund, configured per template | Yes: `merchant_refund.v1` takes `fee_refund`, the part of the refund that comes out of fee revenue. The client decides it for each refund, and the cap stops it exceeding the payment's fee |

**Name change:** the roadmap calls the refund template `refund.v1`. It is
`merchant_refund.v1`, because "refund" is now a generic engine concept
(an entry kind), and the acceptance check from step 2.01 is that no template
name appears in the engine.

Also tested:

- **Money goes back only to whoever paid.** A refund to someone who never
  paid, or one that moves an account the wrong way, is
  `RefundLineNotAllowed` (unit, PostgreSQL, ops).
- **The database refuses on its own.** A tally that shrinks gives `MZ007`, a
  tally above its limit `23514`, and a delete is refused. An over-refund
  written straight to the journal, around the engine, is refused at commit
  with `MZ007`.
- **Idempotency.** A repeated refund returns the original and counts once.
  The same key for a refund of a different entry is `IdempotencyKeyReused`:
  the refunded entry is part of the fingerprint.
- **Disputes.** The disputed money is held until one side wins. Each
  dispute is decided wholly and once. A decision for the payer counts
  against the payment's refund limit, so the payer cannot recover the same
  money by refund and by dispute. A dispute of a reversed payment cannot be
  decided for the payer. The platform keeps its fee when the payer wins.
- **Worked entries.** E11 and E12 (refunds of E08 and E07) and E21–E24 (two
  disputes) are booked by their templates in the full story. Each equals
  its table, and the story ends at the trial balance after E24 (32 tables,
  10,773.20 posted on each side).

## Acceptance criteria

**The over-refund property test passes 10k runs.**
`refunds_never_give_back_more_than_was_paid` (`crates/core/tests/refunds.rs`,
`ProptestConfig::with_cases(10_000)`) takes a random payment (price up to
10,000 ETB, any fee up to 10%) and up to 11 random attempts: refunds of any
split (many over the limit, many small enough to reach it exactly), refunds
to someone who never paid, and reversals. For each attempt, the test
predicts the outcome from its own model, and the reference ledger must
agree. At the end:

- what went back never exceeds the payment, on the payer, the merchant or
  fee revenue;
- what the payer got back equals what the merchant and fee revenue gave;
- refunds and a reversal never both succeed;
- `check_invariants`, including the INV-15 check, holds.

All 10,000 cases pass, in about 8 seconds in a debug build.

## Exit gate

**The dispute policy is written down and reviewed.** It is written down in
[refunds-and-disputes.md](../accounting/refunds-and-disputes.md): the refund
rule, the fee on a refund, the dispute lifecycle, six rules, the behaviour
when the merchant's balance is insufficient, and a list of decisions for the
reviewer. **The review is pending.** Like the accounting review of step
1.01, it needs a person: the product owner, and someone with accounting
knowledge. The step is marked done with the review pending, and the policy's
status line records the reviewer and date once it is reviewed.
