## Exit gate

**Question:** for any table or crate, can you point to the ADR that justifies
it? Seven questions were answered without notes, after one read-through of a
plain-language summary. The answers are recorded as given, with spelling
lightly corrected.

| # | Question | Answer | ADR | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why is `ops` separate from `engine`? | The engine only knows accounting rules, but ops knows how each business event is booked. | 0002 | Pass |
| 2 | Why is money a `BIGINT`, not a float? | Whole numbers are always exact; with `BIGINT` we store cents (santim) easily. | 0005 | Pass |
| 3 | Why is the idempotency key saved in the same transaction as the entry? | To make a payment happen exactly once. | 0008 | Pass, with a gap |
| 4 | Why is there no `proto/` folder? | We chose REST instead of gRPC: most developers know REST, and gRPC needs extra tools without giving us a real benefit. | 0009 | Pass |
| 5 | Why does the account store `debits_total` and `credits_total`? | Adding up millions of lines on every payment is too slow. | 0012 | Pass |
| 6 | Why `reverses_entry_id` instead of editing a wrong entry? | History must never be changed. | 0011 | Pass, with a gap |
| 7 | Why does a withdrawal move money into 2020 instead of a pending flag? | Money waiting for the payout is still owed to the customer, so it must show in the books. | 0004 | Pass |

**Feedback on the gaps:**

- **3:** the answer gives the goal, not why the *same transaction* is needed.
  Saved separately, a crash between the two writes lets a retry post the
  payment twice. In one transaction, both are saved or neither is.
- **6:** the answer gives the goal, not the mechanism. The wrong entry stays,
  and a new mirror entry cancels it, linked by `reverses_entry_id` (E13).

**Result: passed.** Each answer names the right reason for the right
decision. The two gaps are recorded here so they are covered again in steps
1.11 (idempotency) and 1.12 (reversals).
