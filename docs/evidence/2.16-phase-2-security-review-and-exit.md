### The abuse cases

| Case | Abuse | Result |
|---|---|---|
| AC-01 | 33 malformed amounts: signs, exponents, other digits, too many places, numbers, wrong currencies, overflow | All `400` or `422`; no balance moves |
| AC-02 | A balance at `i64::MAX`, then one santim more | The maximum posts; more is refused; nothing wraps |
| AC-03 | The same request ten times at once; the same key with another body or template; another client's key | One entry; `409 idempotency_key_reused`; keys are per client |
| AC-04 | Forty refunds of 5.00 at once against a 100.00 payment with a 2.00 fee; fee refunds larger than the fee | The merchant gives back at most its 98.00, the fee at most its 2.00: exactly 100.00, never a santim more |
| AC-05 | Fifty transfers of 10.00 at once from 100.00 | Exactly ten; the wallet never goes below zero |
| AC-06 | Twenty captures and releases at once on one hold | Exactly one ends it |
| AC-07 | Ten vouchers at once for one device; twenty uploads of the same receipts | One voucher; each receipt pays once; escrows agree |
| AC-08 | Splitting an offline payment to shave the fee | At most a santim saved per split (accepted, F-07) |
| AC-09 | Naming another client's entry in a refund to learn whether it exists | Answered as an entry that does not exist (F-03, fixed) |

## Found in this step

- **F-01, high: unauthenticated work was unbounded.** Inventing a new
  credential ID for each token request passed the per-credential limit and
  cost an Argon2 hash each, and every refusal was a row in the append-only
  audit chain. The first fix checked the budget before the hash and counted
  the failure after; the test that sends 400 logins at once found that all
  400 were hashed, because they all passed the check before the first one
  failed. Reserving first bounds it ([ADR-0021](../adr/0021-unauthenticated-traffic-limits.md)).
- **F-03, medium: found by the fuzzer.** A refund's `refunds` entry was
  looked up without checking whose it was.
- **F-05, medium: the published development key.** Nothing stopped the
  service from signing with `deploy/signing.dev.hex`.
- **The fuzzer's own flaw.** On CI, a small valid request failed to send:
  the fuzzer's pooled client reused a connection the service had just
  closed after refusing an oversized body. The fuzzer now opens a new
  connection per case; the service's behaviour is ordinary HTTP.
